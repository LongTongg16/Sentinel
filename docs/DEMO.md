# Sentinel — 2–3 Minute Demo Guide

A practical script for showing Sentinel live, e.g. in an interview. Written
to sound like you explaining your own project, not a sales pitch.

## 1. What to say in 15 seconds

> "Sentinel is a passive security checker I built. You give it a hostname,
> and it safely inspects the TLS certificate and HTTP security headers a
> site presents — no exploitation, nothing intrusive — then turns that into
> plain-English findings and a scored breakdown of the HTTP header
> configuration."

## 2. What hostname to scan

**`example.com`** is a reasonable suggested target — the screenshots in the
README were captured against it during a prior scan. It is not guaranteed to
behave the same way at demo time: any external site's certificate and
headers can change. **Run a real scan against your chosen hostname shortly
before the demo** so you know what it will actually show. Have a backup
hostname ready (your own site, or another well-known domain) in case the
first one behaves unexpectedly or is unreachable on the day.

## 3. What to point out in TLS results

- The certificate metadata came from an actual verified TLS handshake, not
  a canned response — subject, issuer, validity window, SANs, public key
  type/size are all parsed from the real certificate you just scanned.
- Point out `days_remaining` and the DNS SAN list — this is the kind of
  thing you'd otherwise have to read with `openssl s_client` by hand.
- Whatever the certificate findings actually show is fine to talk through
  as-is: a clean "certificate passed the configured health checks" result
  and a specific problem (e.g. an expiry warning) are both worth explaining
  — say what Sentinel observed for the hostname you scanned, rather than
  assuming a specific outcome in advance.

## 4. What to point out in HTTP headers

- Six specific headers are checked: HSTS, CSP, X-Frame-Options,
  X-Content-Type-Options, Referrer-Policy, Permissions-Policy — each shown
  as present/missing with its actual value.
- Whichever headers come back present or missing for your chosen hostname
  is real, observed behavior — explain what's actually on screen rather
  than a specific result you expected going in.
- The findings list below the headers explains *why* each gap matters in
  plain language, not just "missing".

## 5. How to explain the score

- Point at the score card: a 0–100 number and a letter grade.
- Say explicitly: **this covers four specific controls** — HSTS, framing
  protection, Referrer-Policy, and X-Content-Type-Options — **not** an
  overall "how secure is this site" number. TLS certificate health has no
  numeric score at all; it's assessed separately through its own findings
  and is never folded into the HTTP number.
- Open the deduction breakdown and pick one line, e.g. "Strict-Transport-
  Security header is missing, -20 points" — emphasize that every point lost
  is traceable to a specific control and a specific reason, generated by
  the backend, not guessed by the UI.
- Mention CSP and Permissions-Policy are deliberately **findings-only** —
  say why: the CSP evaluator doesn't yet reproduce enough real CSP grammar
  (nonces, hashes, `strict-dynamic`) to score it responsibly, so it reports
  findings but contributes 0 to the score rather than a guessed value.

## 6. How to explain SSRF protection briefly

> "Before Sentinel connects to anything, it validates the target: no raw IP
> addresses, and every resolved address is checked against private,
> loopback, link-local, and other internal ranges before a connection is
> attempted. It also connects to the specific IP it resolved rather than
> re-resolving the hostname later, and redirects are re-validated the same
> way and capped at a few hops. That's the standard threat model for any
> service that fetches a URL a user gives it — otherwise it can be tricked
> into hitting internal infrastructure."

## 7. One limitation to mention honestly

> "It doesn't do TLS configuration analysis yet — no cipher-suite or
> protocol-version checks, just the leaf certificate itself. And CSP
> scoring is intentionally left out until the evaluator is faithful enough
> to trust."

Saying this unprompted lands better than waiting for someone to find it.

## 8. One strong interview talking point

> "I kept collection, evaluation, and scoring as separate layers on
> purpose — the networking code has no idea what a 'score' is, and the
> scoring code never touches a socket. That separation is why the backend
> has a fully deterministic test suite: every header classifier and
> scoring rule is tested against synthetic header values, with no need to
> hit a real website to verify behavior. It's also why I could make a
> deliberate, documented call to exclude CSP from the numeric score instead
> of guessing at a point value — the methodology text returned by the API
> spells out exactly which controls are scored and why CSP isn't one of
> them."
